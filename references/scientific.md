# Scientific research procedure

Translate the topic into a research question with the population/material/system, intervention or mechanism, comparison, outcome, and relevant conditions where these dimensions fit. For theoretical or historical work, use the field's actual questions rather than forcing a clinical template.

For a broad academic task, begin with relevant reviews to map terminology and key debates, then inspect the original work supporting the important conclusions. Search both foundational literature and later evidence. A recent paper is not automatically more reliable, and citation count is not a quality score.

For a requested systematic or scoping review, agree on or explicitly state databases, dates, inclusion/exclusion criteria, language limits, deduplication and screening methods before screening. Record real counts; do not claim PRISMA compliance, exhaustive coverage, or independent dual screening without actually doing the corresponding work.

## Reading and appraisal

- Verify publication type, authors, date, venue, DOI where available, and article version. Distinguish peer-reviewed publications, conference papers, theses, preprints, reviews, and commentary.
- For pivotal or suspicious sources, check the publisher record and available correction/retraction notices. If publication status cannot be checked, do not claim it has been checked.
- Read methods, results, and relevant limitations for major scientific conclusions whenever full text is accessible. From an abstract, report only what it explicitly supports and label that access limitation.
- Assess design, sample/material, controls, measurements, uncertainty, possible confounding, and generalizability as appropriate. For engineering, record operating conditions, dimensional quantities, model assumptions, and validation. Do not convert association into causation.
- Extract effect sizes, units, and uncertainty when actually reported and relevant. Do not fabricate missing statistics or average incompatible measurements.
- Track study families: a preprint and published paper, multiple analyses of one dataset, and several reviews citing one experiment are not independent confirmations.
- Separate findings from hypotheses and the author's proposed explanation. A journal brand or full-text access does not itself validate a result.

## Expanded evidence appendix

Adapt a compact table to the task:

| Source and year | Design / system / sample | Finding relevant to the question | Limitations and independence | Access level and direct link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then synthesize by subquestion: agreement, disagreement, likely reasons, and what evidence would resolve the uncertainty. Include useful negative results and unsuccessful replications when found. Do not imply that an absence of discovered studies proves an effect is absent.

For reproducibility-focused output, include actual search dates, engines/databases, key query strings and languages, scope filters, inclusion decisions, inaccessible pivotal papers, and reason for stopping. Report counts only when recorded.

Provide bibliographic metadata sufficient to retrieve the cited work. Generate BibTeX or another reference format when requested using verified metadata; leave missing fields out rather than guessing them.
